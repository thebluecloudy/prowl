# Field note: Plastic Halloween buckets (blow mold trick-or-treat pails)

Written in the shape of Prowl's `/research` field note (item, maker, era, marks, repros, sold, notes, sources, solds). Anything the sources did not confirm says "not confirmed".

**Item:** Plastic blow mold Halloween pails: jack-o'-lantern pumpkin buckets (also witches, ghosts, skulls) with a wire or plastic handle, made for trick-or-treating.

**Maker:** Empire Plastics (Empire Blow Form), General Foam Plastics, Union Products (Leominster, MA), and later Carolina Enterprises. Listings and trade coverage name these as the main producers. Many pails carry no maker at all.

**Era:** Halloween blow molds began in the 1950s and 1960s. Most pails shoppers find are 1960s to 1980s. Dated listings include Empire pails stamped 1968 and 1969.

**Marks:** On genuine pieces the maker name, a copyright year, or both are usually embossed into the bottom or back. Look for Empire, General Foam, or Union. Thicker plastic, brush-applied paint details, and soft age wear (yellowing, rubbed paint) are the usual signs of a vintage piece. No mark does not mean fake, since plenty of pails were unmarked. This describes what genuine pails usually show, not any specific pail.

**Repros / lookalikes:** Union Products still makes classic pumpkin pails from its original molds (Walmart lists a current 8 in. one), so a clean, unmarked-looking "classic" pail may be new. Modern copies tend to have thinner plastic and uniform machine paint. Check the embossed year and maker. Not confirmed: exactly which Union molds are the current production runs.

**Sold range:** Not confirmed as a firm range. Search results showed common 1960s-70s jack-o'-lantern pails listed around $12 to $33 (a 5.5 in. 60s-70s jack-o'-lantern pail at $32.99). These are asking prices, not verified sold comps. Large lit yard blow molds and rare figures (Empire Witch on Broom, General Foam Grim Reaper) sell far higher, but those are decor, not pails.

**Notes:** Pails are the cheap entry point into blow mold collecting. Condition drives price: a handle that is original and intact, a bright face, and no cracked or sun-faded plastic matter more than the maker. Check for missing handles, warped plastic from heat, and faded paint. Rarer colors (the pink jack-o'-lantern showed up in listings) and unusual faces are the ones to watch.
