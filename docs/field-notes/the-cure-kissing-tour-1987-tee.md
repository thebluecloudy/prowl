# Field note: The Cure, Kissing Tour 1987 tee (Spring Ford tag)

Written from two photos of the owner's shirt plus web research. This is a note on what the photos show and what pieces like it usually look like. It is not an authentication.

**Item:** The Cure "Kiss Me Kiss Me Kiss Me" tour tee, double-sided. Black shirt.
**Maker:** Neck tag reads Spring Ford Sportswear (as visible in the photo). The licensee and copyright line were not visible in the photos.
**Era:** The back reads "The Cure Kissing Tour 1987", with North American dates beginning July 9 in Vancouver BC, then Seattle, Oakland and San Francisco, and later dates in Chicago, Detroit, Cleveland, Montreal and others (some dates are too small to read). This fits the album, *Kiss Me, Kiss Me, Kiss Me*, released in 1987.

## What the photos show

- **Front:** Hand-lettered "The Cure" in orange and pink, a color-washed Robert Smith portrait in a tilted frame, neon yellow-green slashes, and "Kiss Me Kiss Me Kiss Me" in pink script underneath.
- **Back:** Tour name, year and city dates in yellow-green script, scattered pink, blue and yellow squiggles, and a line of lyric-style text near the bottom ("dancing screaming itching squealing...", partly cut off).
- **Fade:** The black is faded and gray-brown at the shoulders and collar, which is normal for age and wear.
- **Print:** Cracking and wear are visible on the colored areas. The ink looks like it sits on top of the fabric, but I can't judge feel from a photo.

## Marks and labels

- Spring Ford was a general-purpose blank from the late 1970s. By the mid 1980s it was a very common choice for rock tees, and the tag disappeared in the mid 1990s. A Spring Ford tag fits a 1987 tour shirt.
- Spring Ford shirts from this era are often Made in USA, single stitch, and 50/50 cotton-poly. Matching listings for this tour describe a Spring Ford tag in 50/50.
- Still to check on the shirt: the copyright and license line (often small, near the bottom of the front or back print), the tag's fiber content and "Made in USA" line, and single-stitch hems on the sleeves and bottom. The photos don't show these.
- Tag and print should agree. Fakers sew old-style tags onto modern blanks, so a Spring Ford tag alone is not proof.

## Repros / lookalikes

- The Cure is a heavily faked brand, and the *Kiss Me* artwork has been reprinted. Check that the hems are single stitch, the print feels rubbery and raised, and the tag wording and font are era-correct.
- The photos show nothing that conflicts with a period piece. They also can't confirm one.

## Sold range

- Search results listed a vintage 1987 Kiss Me tour tee at $225 asking. Other 1987 Kissing Tour listings (XL, M) showed no price in the results.
- The Hypebeast dealer interviews put The Cure tees generally at $150 to $250. Wish Tour and Disintegration shirts are said to sell higher, but I couldn't verify those sales.
- Treat all of these as asking prices and dealer ranges, not confirmed sold comps. Check eBay sold listings for the last six months before pricing.

## Notes

- Back dates make this a tour shirt rather than an album-only shirt, which helps the story and the appeal.
- Condition drives value: look for holes, pit stains, print flaking, and whether the fabric feels crispy. Dry rot is a risk with older black tees, and the dark fade here is normal but worth a close look.
- Size and measurements matter to buyers. Add pit-to-pit and length when listing.
