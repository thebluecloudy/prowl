# Vintage band tees: a collector's field guide

Vintage band tees are concert and promo shirts from the 1970s to the 1990s, hunted for their original tour graphics, faded softness, and tie to a specific album or tour.

**Era:** Late 1970s to mid-1990s. Single-stitch hems point to roughly the late 1970s through the early 1990s. Around 1994 to 1995 the industry moved to double-needle stitching.
**Maker:** Licensed merchandisers such as Brockum and Winterland are the names collectors look for. Blanks include Screen Stars, Giant, Hanes and Stedman. Bands used different suppliers depending on the show, so a brand name alone does not prove a shirt.
**Materials:** 100% cotton or 50/50 cotton-poly, single-stitch sleeve and bottom hems, screen-printed graphics.
**Typical price range:** Not confirmed as one range. One tag database puts single-stitch Screen Stars tees at $20 to $150 on eBay, with desirable band graphics reaching $80 to $200 in good condition. Sought-after tour shirts can run well above that.
**Why collectable:** Limited tour production, era-specific art, and lasting fashion demand.

## How to recognize it

- **Hems:** One line of stitching on the sleeves and bottom suggests late 1970s to early 1990s. Double-needle stitching on a shirt sold as an 80s tour tee is a red flag.
- **Ink:** Old plastisol ink sits on top of the fabric with a stiff or rubbery feel and cracks with age. If the print is as soft as the fabric, it may be a recent water-based or digital print.
- **Fabric:** A thin, washed weight with some narrowing from age is typical. Dry rot, crispy fabric, or a syrupy smell is a condition problem.

## Makers, marks and labels

- Look for a copyright line and licensee printed on the shirt, such as a year plus "Under License to" a merchandiser, and check that it fits the tag.
- Era tags in tag databases and collector forums include Brockum, Winterland, Giant, Screen Stars, Stedman and Hanes.
- Tags from the 1980s are often a stiff, paper-like polyester that goes brittle and translucent. Study how the tag feels, not just the logo.
- This describes what genuine shirts usually show, not any specific shirt.

## Fakes and lookalikes

- Sellers sew "vintage" Screen Stars or Giant tags onto modern blanks. A good tag on its own proves nothing.
- Check that the print's copyright year and licensee match the tag and the stitching. Mismatched fonts or spelling errors signal a fake.
- Gildan, Next Level, or double-stitched shirts sold as 80s tour tees are not confirmed vintage.
- New "vintage style" shirts and official band-store re-releases carry new tags and are not vintage.
- Ask for photos of the front, back, tag and hem stitching before buying.

## Condition and care

Honest wear is fine. Cracking is normal. Dry rot and holes cost value. Wash inside out in cold water and air dry. Fold instead of hanging so the neck and shoulders do not stretch.

## Where to find it

Estate sales, thrift stores, flea markets, and eBay, Depop, Etsy and Grailed. Search eBay sold listings first to see what a graphic has really sold for.
